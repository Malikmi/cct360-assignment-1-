Arthur Blackwood, 71, started his own company, Blackwood & Co., and became very wealthy. Tonight he was found dead in the study of his home, Blackwood Manor.

The police think he died of natural causes and have already left, but you are the detective on the case and you aren't so sure. The study door was locked, there was a glass next to his body, and his safe was open with half of a letter missing.

The family is staying in the house tonight because of the storm. They plan to leave at dawn, so you have until morning to find out what really happened.

Case Closed

The police take Julian away. You put the will on the desk as the last piece of proof:

Last Will and Testament of Arthur Blackwood

I, Arthur Blackwood, being of sound mind, revoke all previous wills.

To my daughter, Victoria, and my son, Edward: five thousand pounds each.

To my nephew, Julian Blackwood, who has run the company with such loyalty: Blackwood Manor, all my shares in Blackwood & Co., and the rest of my estate.

Signed, Arthur Blackwood. Tuesday, the day before he died.

Arthur signed this will on Tuesday. On Wednesday he found out that Julian had stolen £400,000 from the company, so he booked his lawyer for 9 AM on Thursday to undo it.

Julian only had that one night to stop him. He carried the brandy up himself, locked the study with the spare key, burned half of Arthur's letter to the lawyer, and washed his own glass.

If Arthur made it to the meeting, Julian would lose everything. So he made sure Arthur never got there.
